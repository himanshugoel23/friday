# India's ageing population: demographics, living situation, money, digital behaviour, payers and caregivers (as of 2026-11-12)

Conventions: every statistic carries its survey or publication date. [U] means not verified from a primary or reputable source in this research pass. Several tool fetches hit paywalls or 403s, so some figures come from press coverage of primary reports; this is flagged per line. Market-size numbers are vendor or industry estimates and should be treated with caution.

## 1. How many Indians are 60+ (2026, 2030, 2036, 2050), fastest-ageing states, dependency, speed vs Japan/China/Korea

### Takeaway
India has roughly 150-167 million people aged 60+ (about 10.5-11.5%) depending on source and year, heading to roughly 191 million by 2030, about 15% (about 227 million) by 2036 and about 347 million (about 20.8%) by 2050. The 60+ group grows several times faster than the total population. Ageing is concentrated first in the south, Himachal and Punjab.

### Cited Findings
- UNFPA India Ageing Report 2023 (released 2023-09-28): 60+ population 149 million in 2022, rising to 347 million in 2050; share about 10% (2021) to about 15% (2036) to 20.8% (2050). 2036 equals about 22.7 crore. Coverage notes the report draws on LASI 2017-18, Census, the Government of India projections (2011-2036) and UN WPP 2022 — [ETV Bharat summary](https://www.etvbharat.com/english/bharat/young-india-will-rapidly-turn-into-grey-population-in-coming-decades-says-unfpa-report/na20230927174048096096174); [UNFPA India](https://india.unfpa.org/en/news/india-ageing-elderly-make-20-population-2050-unfpa-report)
- UNFPA: elderly decadal growth rate currently about 41%; 80+ population projected to grow about 279% between 2022 and 2050; the elderly are projected to outnumber children (0-15) by 2046 (some outlets differ on the year) — [UNFPA India](https://india.unfpa.org/en/news/india-ageing-elderly-make-20-population-2050-unfpa-report)
- UNFPA India chief (2024-07): 60+ growth of about 134% from 2022 to 2050 against 18% overall population growth; 346 million cited for 2050 — press coverage of UNFPA statement, [Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/amp/india-news/elderly-population-in-india-likely-to-double-by-2050-unfpa-india-chief-124072100120_1.html) (via search snippet, page not opened)
- JLL insight "India's silver economy: from niche to necessity" (published 2026-09-07; authors Rohan Sharma, Madhurima Basu, Ketan Bhingarde, Aryan Govindakrishnan): 60+ about 166.9 million today, 191.5 million by 2030, 346 million by 2050; 75+ is about a quarter of seniors and grows at 7.8% CAGR to 2030 — [JLL India](https://www.jll.com/en-in/insights/india-silver-economy-from-niche-to-necessity). Note the 166.9M is higher than UNFPA's 149M (2022) and the NITI figure below; definitions and base-year differ.
- NITI Aayog "Senior Care Reforms in India" (Feb 2024): seniors a little over 10% of population, about 104 million (this is the Census 2011 base per summaries, so it is dated); only about 34% of older people still working — summaries via [Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/senior-care-reforms-in-india-niti-aayog/print_manually); NITI PDF returned 403, so primary not read.
- States: in 2021 most southern states plus Himachal Pradesh and Punjab were above the national average elderly share, with the gap widening by 2036; Bihar and UP rise but stay below average. Old-age dependency (elderly per 100 aged 15-59): south about 20, west 17, UTs 13, northeast 13 — [UNFPA India](https://india.unfpa.org/en/news/india-ageing-elderly-make-20-population-2050-unfpa-report). One outlet says about one in five will be 60+ in southern states by 2036 — [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/english/bharat/young-india-will-rapidly-turn-into-grey-population-in-coming-decades-says-unfpa-report/na20230927174048096096174)
- Life expectancy at 60: women about 23 more years in Himachal and 22 in Kerala; elderly sex ratio rose in central India from 973 (2011) to 1,053 (2021) — [UNFPA India](https://india.unfpa.org/en/news/india-ageing-elderly-make-20-population-2050-unfpa-report)
- Total population (UN WPP 2024, released July 2024): 1.45 billion in 2024, peaking around 1.69-1.7 billion in the 2050s-early 2060s, then declining — [The Print](https://theprint.in/india/indias-population-to-peak-at-1-7-billion-in-next-35-years-before-declining-by-12-un-report/2175194) (snippet only)
- Other: over 19,500 Indians turn 60 each day (citing ASLI) — [The Ken, 2026-10-29](https://the-ken.com/story/india-needs-about-2-million-caregivers-the-govt-has-trained-fewer-than-40000/)

### Inferences
- A defensible planning range for 60+ is about 150-165 million in 2026, about 190 million in 2030, about 225 million in 2036, about 345 million in 2050. The 2036 and 2050 shares are UNFPA-consistent; the 2026 absolute count is source-dependent (+/- 10%).
- Dependency ratio is about 17-20 per 100 in south/west; since 60+ more than doubles by 2050 while 15-59 plateaus, the national ratio will roughly double (inference, not a sourced figure).
- Cross-country speed: from general knowledge, Japan took about 24 years to go from 7% to 14% aged 65+, Korea about 18, China about 25-ish, and India is projected to take roughly 28 years or more [U] — I could not retrieve the comparison table (the IPSS Japan table at https://www.ipss.go.jp/syoushika/tohkei/Data/Popular2023/T02-18.files/sheet001.htm lists years-to-double by country and is the right primary source). The qualitative point, that India ages at similar speed but at far lower income per capita than those countries, is widely made but not sourced here [U].

### Gaps
- No primary UN WPP 2024 numbers for 60+ in 2030/2036/2050 (only press and UNFPA/JLL). No state-wise ranking of ageing speed beyond the qualitative list. Japan/China/Korea doubling-time numbers not verified. NITI Aayog and UNFPA PDFs not directly opened (403/paywall).

## 2. Living arrangements

### Takeaway
Most Indian elders still live with children (LASI 2017-18), but 5.7% of 60+ live alone and 20.3% live with spouse and/or others without children; widowhood affects 36% of 60+ (much higher for women). Those shares are rising with migration, and no newer national wave was found.

### Cited Findings
- LASI Wave 1 (fieldwork Apr 2017-Dec 2018; 73,396 adults 45+ and spouses; 31,902 aged 60+ interviewed), IIPS fact sheet. For ages 60+: living alone 5.7% (45-59: 1.4%); living with spouse and/or others (no children) 20.3%; living with spouse and children 40.6%; living with children and others 27.6%; living with others only 5.7%; widowed 36.2% (45-59: 9.4%); currently married 61.6%; satisfied with living arrangement 74.8% — [LASI W1 India fact sheet](https://www.iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/other_files/LASI_W1_Factsheet_INDIA.pdf) (read directly via pdftotext). Note: the fact sheet's Male/Female/Rural/Urban columns cover all 45+, not 60+ only, so the 60+ by sex/residence split is not available from it. For all 45+: living alone 3.9% of women vs 1.8% of men (the columns read male 1.8, female 4.5 for living alone per row layout; treat as indicative).
- A secondary summary of the same fact sheet gives about 9% of women 60+ living alone and 54% of women 60+ widowed [U, snippet only; not confirmed in the fact sheet text I read] — [search summary](https://www.iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/other_files/LASI_W1_Factsheet_INDIA.pdf)
- LASI-based study: widowed older adults living alone were 56% more likely to be depressed than married co-residing peers (AOR 1.56; CI 1.28-1.91) — [PMC8568934](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8568934) (snippet only)
- UNFPA 2023: older women more likely widowed, alone, with no income and family-dependent — [UNFPA India](https://india.unfpa.org/en/news/india-ageing-elderly-make-20-population-2050-unfpa-report)
- LASI W1: about 24% of older adults struggle with at least one daily activity — cited in [The Ken, 2026-10-29](https://the-ken.com/story/india-needs-about-2-million-caregivers-the-govt-has-trained-fewer-than-40000/)

### Inferences
- "Alone or with spouse only" is about 26% of 60+ (5.7% + 20.3%) in 2017-18, which is about 40+ million people on a 150-165M base (derived arithmetic). Likely higher by 2026 [U].
- 73% of elders being in a household with children means the adult child (often in a metro or abroad) is the buyer/decision-maker for many products.

### Gaps
- No urban vs rural 60+ split, NRI-family share, Census 2011 or NSSO 75th round (2017-18) numbers, or LASI Wave 2 results found. NITI Aayog living-arrangements data not read (403). Trend over time not sourced.

## 3. Income, pensions, insurance, silver-economy size

### Takeaway
Formal pension coverage is thin (roughly 6-9% have work-related pension or PF; about 29% have any pension/PF/social scheme per HelpAge 2024), about a third have no income, and health insurance for seniors is mostly public (Ayushman). Spending power sits in a small urban affluent segment (JLL: about 1.7 million senior households in 2026). Market-size numbers range widely by definition.

### Cited Findings
- LASI W1 (2017-18), 60+: covered under work-related pension scheme 8.6%; covered by Provident Fund 6.0%; officially retired from organised sector 7.7%; currently receiving retirement pension 7.7% (rows 56-59, ages 60+) — [LASI fact sheet](https://www.iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/other_files/LASI_W1_Factsheet_INDIA.pdf)
- LASI W1 household level (all households with age-eligible members): any health insurance 26.7% (RSBY and allied 20.6%, CGHS/ESIS 3.6%, employer reimbursement 1.2%, private commercial 1.3%); per capita annual household income INR 44,726 (rural 35,710; urban 65,608); mean household pension income INR 3,604 per year; 60+ currently working 35.7% — [same fact sheet](https://www.iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/other_files/LASI_W1_Factsheet_INDIA.pdf)
- HelpAge India "Ageing in India" (released 2024-06-14): only 29% of elders have access to social security (old-age pension, contributory pension, PF); one in three earned no income in the past year; 65% reported not being financially secure — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/only-29-elderly-have-access-to-social-security-schemes-like-pension-pf-study/2131998), [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/socialstory/2024/06/elders-not-financially-secure-report-helpage-inida) (snippets; primary PDF 404)
- UNFPA 2023: over 40% of elderly are in the poorest wealth quintile; about 18.7% have no income — [UNFPA India](https://india.unfpa.org/en/news/india-ageing-elderly-make-20-population-2050-unfpa-report)
- Ayushman Vay Vandana (AB-PMJAY cover for all 70+, INR 5 lakh per family per year, launched Oct 2024): 93 lakh+ cards as of 2025-12-05 (Rajya Sabha reply); 1.14 crore as of 2026-02-28; about 1.36 crore as of 2026-09-21; target population cited at about 6 crore [U on denominator] — [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/amp/news/health/hospital-diagnostics/36229-hospitals-empanelled-435-crore-cards-issued-under-ab-pmjay-166744), [Ommcom/Rajya Sabha reply](https://ommcomnews.com/india-news/over-93-lakh-ayushman-cards-issued-for-senior-citizens-over-age-70-issued-till-dec-govt/), [Magzter/Statesman](https://www.magzter.com/ja/stories/newspaper/The-Statesman/VAY-VANDANA-CARDS-ACHIEVES-MILESTONE) (snippets)
- JLL/ASLI (2026-09-07): about 1.7 million urban financially independent senior households in 2026, rising to 2.1 million in 2030; organised senior housing stock 25,050 units (June 2026), 1.5% penetration vs 6-7% US; 2030 scenarios 51,000 / 63,000 / 74,000 units with capex INR 43,200 / 58,800 / 73,100 crore (about USD 4.5 / 6.2 / 7.7B); "policy-led boom" opportunity about USD 10.1B — [JLL India](https://www.jll.com/en-in/insights/india-silver-economy-from-niche-to-necessity). Method: housing-stock scenario analysis, real-estate-led; senior housing only, not total silver economy.
- The Ken (2026-10-29), citing ASLI: senior care market (home care plus senior residences) currently USD 10-15B, expected USD 30-50B over the next decade — [The Ken](https://the-ken.com/story/india-needs-about-2-million-caregivers-the-govt-has-trained-fewer-than-40000/) (paywalled; basis not visible)
- Press: "USD 50B silver economy by 2030 at ~20% CAGR" and "silver economy managing USD 1.5T assets by 2030 (CII-BCG framework)" — [Realty n More](https://realtynmore.com/indias-silver-economy-a-50-billion-market-opportunity-by-2030/) [U, secondary, methodology unknown]
- NITI Aayog 2024 recommends silver-economy promotion (SAGE initiative), GST/tax relief on senior products, affordable devices, CSR/PPP for care infrastructure — [Drishti IAS summary](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/senior-care-reforms-in-india-niti-aayog/print_manually)

### Inferences
- The reachable paying segment is small relative to 150M+: urban independent senior households (about 1.7M) is the only sourced affluent count. Most seniors' purchases are family-funded (see Q5).
- Market-size claims differ by 5x (USD 10B senior-living vs USD 50B broad); do not quote a single "silver economy" number without definition.

### Gaps
- No PFRDA/NPS/APY coverage numbers for 60+, no EPFO pensioner counts, no RBI/PFRDA data fetched. No data on share financially dependent on children (HelpAge/LASI exist but not retrieved). No private health insurance penetration for 60+ post-2018. Silver-economy estimates from NITI/CII-BCG not read at source.

## 4. Digital behaviour of 60+

### Takeaway
Roughly 40-45% of 60+ have smartphone access, a majority still rely partly on basic phones, and the main barrier is confidence rather than access once a person is online. WhatsApp is the dominant app; no reliable national numbers on voice vs typing or language preference were found.

### Cited Findings
- NSS Comprehensive Modular Survey on Telecom 2025 (80th round), analysed in Dey & Paul (2026): smartphone access 44.7% (60-69), 42% (70+), 57.9% (50-59); about 2 in 5 of 60+ can use the internet through any device; more than half rely wholly or partly on basic/feature phones; 97%+ of those with smartphone access used it recently; nearly 9 in 10 older internet users go online daily; online banking ability 53.1% of 60+ (vs 71.4% of 18-49; men 57%, women 46%); only 8% of 60+ (6% women, 4% rural) can report cybercrime on the national portal — [Ideas for India](https://www.ideasforindia.in/topics/governance/making-indias-digital-public-infrastructure-work-for-older-citizens)
- HelpAge India intergenerational survey (released 2025-06-13; 5,798 respondents in 10 cities, 30% aged 60+): 40% of elders own a smartphone, 71% use basic phones, 13% use computers/social media/internet, 66% find digital tools too confusing, 51% avoid digital modes for fear of errors, Digital Inclusion Index 50.71/100; no UPI/WhatsApp/language data — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/news/digital-divide-among-elderly-66-find-digital-tools-too-confusing). Urban-only sample, so not national.
- HelpAge India 2024 national report: 41% of elders have access to any digital device, 59% none; men 48% vs women 33%; only 26% of 80+; about 1 in 5 can use devices comfortably; smartphones most common device — [HelpAge press release](https://www.helpageindia.org/news/weaad-2024-press-release) (snippet)
- HelpAge 2022 ("Bridge the Gap"): among those with phones, use was calls 49%, social media 30%, banking 17% — search snippet [U on exact framing]
- News18 survey of 2,002 urban Indians 55+: 76.9% use smartphones (urban only) and a 2018 mobile survey with 94% WhatsApp use — both secondary blog citations [U]
- Language: a Pune qualitative study notes mixed-language interfaces overwhelm older users and fonts/jargon are barriers — [AcademicJobs summary](https://www.academicjobs.com/in/research-publication-news/digital-literacy-indian-seniors-pune-nature-study-or-academicjobs-11535) [U, secondary]

### Inferences
- Reachable-by-smartphone 60+ is about 40-45% nationally, so about 60-70 million people in 2026 (derived from 150-165M base); roughly 80M reachable by voice phone call only. This supports a voice-call-first channel (as Friday does) over an app-first channel.
- Online banking ability at 53% among older internet users suggests UPI comfort exists within the connected group.

### Gaps
- No GSMA, IAMAI/Kantar, or LASI digital data retrieved. No sourced numbers for WhatsApp/YouTube/UPI among 60+, voice vs typing, or regional-language preference.

## 5. Who pays for elder care and typical spend

### Takeaway
Family (adult children) funds most care; government supplies thin pensions and Ayushman cover for 70+; organised private spend is concentrated among affluent urban households and NRIs. Hard monthly-spend benchmarks were mostly not found.

### Cited Findings
- HelpAge 2024: about 70% of elderly depend on family or pension for day-to-day maintenance; 65% say they are not financially secure — [Governance Now](https://www.governancenow.com/news/regular-story/70-of-indias-elderly-depend-on-family-or-pension-for-daytoday-maintenance-helpage-report) (snippet)
- LASI W1: mean household pension income only INR 3,604/year; government transfers INR 1,500/year (fact sheet) — [LASI fact sheet](https://www.iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/other_files/LASI_W1_Factsheet_INDIA.pdf)
- Ayushman Vay Vandana (government) covers 70+ regardless of income (see Q3).
- Agency example: a Bengaluru 24-hour live-in caregiver earned INR 24,000/month of which agency kept INR 5,000 (single anecdote) — [The Ken](https://the-ken.com/story/india-needs-about-2-million-caregivers-the-govt-has-trained-fewer-than-40000/)
- NITI Aayog suggests CSR and PPP for care infrastructure (CSR is a proposed, not established, payer) — [Drishti summary](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/senior-care-reforms-in-india-niti-aayog/print_manually)

### Inferences
- Realistic payer hierarchy for a consumer product: adult child (local or NRI) first, senior's own savings second, insurers/employers/CSR third (inference).

### Gaps
- No data on NRI remittance for parent care, employer eldercare benefits, insurer (OPD/home-care) products, or monthly spend by service (home nurse, assisted living, day care). Pricing for assisted living not retrieved.

## 6. Caregiver and workforce supply

### Takeaway
Trained geriatric caregivers are scarce: estimates of need run from about 2 million (The Ken, Oct 2026) to 4.3 million (other press), against under 40,000 government-trained. Wage evidence is anecdotal.

### Cited Findings
- India needs about 2 million caregivers; government has trained fewer than 40,000; senior-care firms offering hospital-level pay, food and housing still cannot hire enough (The Ken, 2026-10-29) — [The Ken](https://the-ken.com/story/india-needs-about-2-million-caregivers-the-govt-has-trained-fewer-than-40000/)
- Other press: shortfall of about 4.3 million trained caregivers vs about 36,000 available; a 2025 conference cited only 5,000-6,000 trained [U, conflicting, secondary] — [Devdiscourse](https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/headlines/3526055-bridging-the-care-gap-indias-urgent-need-for-professional-elderly-caregivers)
- Training for formal caregivers in geriatric mental health and dementia has no minimum standards — [PMC11712114](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11712114/)
- India has about 0.9 hospital beds per 1,000 vs WHO 3.5 — secondary 2023 analysis [U] (snippet of search results)

### Inferences
- The shortage supports tech that extends family or low-skilled caregiver capacity (check-ins, reminders, companionship); wage anecdote (INR 24,000/month live-in) suggests human 24x7 care is unaffordable for most households.

### Gaps
- No nurse-per-1,000 density from WHO/NHP, no attendant-bureau market size, no sector wage survey, no official shortage projection (the 2M vs 4.3M figures conflict and methods are unknown).
