# Problems of older Indians (60+) and their caring children: evidence table, as of 2026-11-12

Method note: about 17 web searches, mostly search-result summaries. I opened no primary PDFs. Every number below comes from a search-result summary that points to the source URL. Anything that rests on a news outlet, blog or vendor page, or that I could not tie to a primary paper, is marked [U]. Dates are the survey or data year, not the publication year. LASI = Longitudinal Ageing Study in India, wave 1, fieldwork 2017-18.

## 1. Loneliness, social isolation, depression (with Japan/Korea comparison)

### Takeaway
About 13-15% of Indians 60+ report frequent loneliness (LASI wave 1, 2017-18), and about 8-9% screen positive for major depression. Living alone, depression and poor health predict loneliness. Only about one-fifth of the elderly live alone or with a spouse only, so the problem is concentrated in a minority of households, but that minority is growing. Japan's solitary-death statistics show where this goes at scale; India has no equivalent count.

### Cited Findings
- 30,394 adults aged 60+ in LASI wave 1: 13.4% reported frequent loneliness; the state-level maximum was in Andaman and Nicobar and the minimum in Tripura. — [LASI wave-1 loneliness paper (library record)](https://library.kab.ac.ug/Record/doaj-art-e52cc386d87b412b92a066f88535a0f9)
- A second LASI analysis reports 14.95% of elderly feeling lonely, higher in women. — [Mahidol repository](https://repository.li.mahidol.ac.th/handle/123456789/84804)
- Age 45+ (about 72,000 respondents, single-item measure): 20.5% moderate and 13.3% severe loneliness. — [IJCMPH](https://www.ijcmph.com/index.php/ijcmph/article/download/15298/9129/76521)
- Conference abstract [U]: 18% of adults 45+ lonely, 20% of women vs 15% of men, 27% among those 75+. Predictors: living alone (adjusted odds ratio 2.8), depressive symptoms (2.4), poor self-rated health (1.9), multimorbidity (1.6). Not peer reviewed. — [IAFOR abstract](https://submit.iafor.org/submission/submission103106/)
- Depression in 60+, CIDI-SF, LASI wave 1 (n = 31,464): 8.67% screened as depressed; other papers cite about 8.3%. Age-standardised depression for 45+ was 5.7%, against only 0.5% self-reported diagnosis, so about 90% of depression is undiagnosed. I did not open the full text. — [BMC Psychiatry / Springer](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s12888-022-03959-3.pdf); [BMC Public Health](https://bmcpublichealth.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12889-022-14745-x.pdf)
- UNFPA India Ageing Report 2023: about 149 million aged 60+ in 2022 (10.5%), projected 347 million (20.8%) by 2050. Older population grows 134% 2022-2050. About one-fifth of the elderly live alone or with a spouse only. This is LASI 2017-18 data, and "alone" is not separated from "spouse only". — [Drishti IAS summary](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/india-ageing-report-2023); [UNFPA India](https://india.unfpa.org/en/news/india-ageing-elderly-make-20-population-2050-unfpa-report) [U] on exact split
- HelpAge India 2025 intergenerational report: 83% of elders live with, or plan to live with, family (secondary report). — [HelpAge India](https://www.helpageindia.org/news/helpage-india-report-calls-for-strengthening-intergenerational-bonds/) [U]
- Japan: police recorded 76,020 people who died alone at home in 2024, about 76% aged 65+ (one outlet gives 58,044 for 65+; unreconciled). The 2025 figure was reported as 76,941. About 22,000 of the 2024 cases were found after 8+ days ("isolated deaths"), with about 80% men. — [Morung Express (2024 figure)](https://morungexpress.com/over-76000-people-die-alone-in-japan-in-2024); [The Star, 2026-04-25](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/04/25/japans-lonely-deaths-hit-76000---thailand-at-risk-of-following); [Asiae](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2025041212540975912) [U] for all, since they are news reports of National Police Agency data
- South Korea: 3,661 solitary deaths in 2023 and 3,378 in 2021, under the solitary-deaths prevention act in force since 2021-04-01. — [Megacurioso](https://www.megacurioso.com.br/estilo-de-vida/kodokushi-e-godoksa-as-mortes-solitarias-no-japao-e-na-coreia-do-sul) [U]

### Inferences
- Loneliness is frequent but diffuse. It is real in about 1 in 7 elders, and the strongest predictors (living alone, depression, multimorbidity) identify a targetable subgroup.
- Because 80%+ of Indian elders live with family, Japan-style solo death is a smaller tail risk today. It is a trend to watch: urban migration of children, widowed women living alone.
- Japan and Korea definitions differ, so do not compare their counts directly with each other.

### Gaps
- No Indian count of "found dead after days alone". NCRB does not appear to publish one; I found none.
- No 60+ living-alone percentage that separates alone from spouse-only. Needs the LASI or UNFPA full report.
- Health effects of loneliness in India (mortality, dementia risk): I did not search for Indian estimates.

## 2. Falls, injuries, hip fracture, home-alone emergencies

### Takeaway
Roughly 10-13% of Indians 60+ reported a fall in the past two years and about 5-6% a fall injury needing care (LASI wave 1). Hip fracture one-year mortality is about 19% in Indian cohorts, and most costs are out of pocket. Time-to-discovery after a fall has no national data.

### Cited Findings
- LASI wave 1, 60+: fall 12.63%, fall-injury 5.64%, multiple falls 5.76% (state-wise table); falls are self-reported over two years. — [PMC table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9816101/table/Tab2)
- n = 28,285: frail adults had more falls (15.43% vs 11.85%), multiple falls (7.73% vs 5.25%), fall injuries (6.68% vs 5.29%). — [PMC9374260](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9374260/)
- n = 31,477: 12.9% had one fall and 9.8% recurrent falls (2+) in two years (about 22.7% combined; I added this). Rural 12.5% vs urban 9.5% in another analysis. Another LASI paper reports 20.25% (n = 31,345). Estimates therefore range roughly 10-23% by definition. — [BMC Public Health](https://bmcpublichealth.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12889-022-14697-2.pdf)
- Review-based range of 14-53% for falls in older Indians, not LASI. — same BMC source [U]
- Hip fracture: incidence "over 120 per 100,000 aged 50+" (review estimate) [U]; the same source notes India lacks reliable fragility-fracture data. — [BMJ blog](https://blogs.bmj.com/bmj/2017/11/21/implementing-best-practice-guidance-for-management-of-older-people-with-hip-fractures-in-india/)
- One-year mortality after hip fracture: 19.0% in one consecutive series; 18.92% in a Jharkhand referral centre; 7.7% in one military hospital (single-site). — [JOCR 2025](https://jocr.co.in/wp/2025/02/demographic-insights-into-one-year-mortality-following-proximal-femur-fracture-surgery-in-india/); [PMC10755790](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10755790)
- Delayed surgery in 69.3% of patients in a Bhubaneswar multicentre study, linked to pressure sores, infection and cost; another study found no mortality difference. Costs mostly out of pocket, not insurance. — [PMC10505843](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505843)

### Inferences
- With about 149 million 60+ (UNFPA, 2022), 5-6% fall injury per two years implies several million injurious falls every two years. This is my arithmetic, not a published number.
- The severe end (hip fracture) is rare per person but costly and deadly, and families pay directly.

### Gaps
- No national annual fall incidence (LASI is two-year recall).
- No cost-per-hip-fracture figure in rupees found. No data on time lying undiscovered, or on deaths from falls nationally.
- WHO falls data not retrieved.

## 3. Chronic disease, medication adherence, follow-ups

### Takeaway
Hypertension and diabetes are common and mostly unmanaged. In LASI 45+, 55.7% of hypertensives were diagnosed, 38.9% on treatment and 31.7% controlled (one analysis; another says only 10.4% controlled). Adherence studies are small and range 24-73% adherent. Cost, irregular supply and forgetfulness recur.

### Cited Findings
- Hypertension cascade, LASI 45+ (not 60+): 55.7% aware, 38.9% treated, 31.7% controlled; biggest loss at awareness (45.3 percentage points). — [PMC8726460](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8726460/)
- Conflicting analysis of self-reported hypertension: only 10.4% controlled. Definitions differ. — [Frontiers](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2021.766458/full)
- 66,470 adults 45+: 51.3% of biochemical diabetes and 63.8% of measured hypertension undiagnosed. — [BMC Health Services Research](https://bmchealthservres.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12913-023-09318-6.pdf)
- About 20% of adults 45+ had diabetes in 2019, nearly 40% of them unaware. — [India Med Today, 2026-03](https://indiamedtoday.com/indias-ageing-population-signals-rising-diabetes-burden-among-adults-over-45/) [U]
- Multimorbidity: about one-third of older adults have multiple chronic conditions, strong age and socioeconomic gradient (LASI 60+, n = 31,386). — [BMC Geriatrics](https://bmcgeriatr.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12877-023-04264-8.pdf)
- Adherence: Udupi, Karnataka (2023, n = 360 rural elderly with hypertension and type 2 diabetes): 55.6% non-adherent, linked to medicine cost. — [Patient Preference and Adherence](https://doaj.org/article/21d928c6dda84433b6e2213d8a4033dd)
- Rural West Bengal geriatric hypertensives (2018): reasons for poor adherence were irregular government pharmacy supply 72.3%, forgetfulness 67.5%, unaffordability 66.3%. — [Natl J Community Med](https://doaj.org/article/36809bc322974bda88bdd7e80765a08a)
- Adherence 24% (rural Kancheepuram) to 73% (Kolkata slums), reviewed in one paper; tertiary hospital 45+ study: poor adherence 35.5%. All single-site. — [IJCMPH](https://www.ijcmph.com/index.php/ijcmph/article/download/14295/8627/71283)

### Inferences
- Forgetfulness is a top self-reported driver, and it is addressable by reminders, which fits a phone-call product. Cost and supply are not addressable that way.
- Undiagnosed disease means the first gap is screening, not reminders.

### Gaps
- No national adherence figure for 60+. No polypharmacy prevalence for 60+ found. No missed-follow-up rate found.

## 4. Dementia and cognitive decline

### Takeaway
About 7.4-8.4% of Indians 60+ have dementia (about 8.8 million per LASI-DAD), and most cases are undiagnosed. Caregiving cost is borne by families, with high caregiver distress.

### Cited Findings
- LASI-DAD nationally representative estimate: 7.4% of 60+, about 8.8 million people. — [Alzheimers data / LASI-DAD](https://www.alzheimersdata.org/lasi-dad); [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/neurology-neurosurgery/news/present-status-and-future-risk-of-rising-dementia-cases-in-india-cause-of-concern-107645)
- Separate machine-learning estimate on LASI wave 1: 8.44% (95% CI 7.89-9.01%), age-standardised 8.94%. This is not LASI-DAD. — [Karger Neuroepidemiology](https://karger.com/ned/article/57/1/43/832546/Estimating-the-Prevalence-of-Dementia-in-India)
- ARDSI "Dementia in India 2020": 3.7 million in 2010, 5.29 million projected 2020 (older projection, below LASI-DAD). — [PARI / Rural India Online](https://ruralindiaonline.org/en/library/resource/dementia-in-india-2020)
- Household annual care cost (2019): about Rs 29,000-96,000 rural, Rs 66,000-3 lakh urban. — same ARDSI source; [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/health/cover/2023/07/29/the-lonely-journey-of-dementia-caregivers.amp.html) (slightly different bounds; check the report)
- 10/66 study cited by The Week: 40-72% of primary caregivers report high psychological morbidity. — same The Week link [U]

### Inferences
- Dementia is the highest-severity condition here, but diagnosis gaps mean current paying demand is below the true need. Caregivers are the buyer.

### Gaps
- Diagnosis rate not found. Caregiver hours per week not found. Projected 2050 dementia number not retrieved.

## 5. Financial fraud and crimes against seniors

### Takeaway
Fraud is the best-measured and most monetised problem. In 2025, 1,03,488 senior citizens filed financial cyber-fraud complaints on NCRP with Rs 4,005 crore lost. Reporting is partial.

### Cited Findings
- MHA reply to Rajya Sabha, 2025: 1,03,488 senior citizens and 4,63,114 women filed financial cyber-fraud complaints totalling Rs 7,769.63 crore; senior citizens lost Rs 4,005.12 crore. The two groups probably overlap, so do not add them. — [Asianet Newsable](https://newsable.asianetnews.com/india/senior-citizens-women-lost-rs-7-769-crore-to-cyber-fraud-in-2025-articleshow-u9oad8w) [U] (media report of official reply)
- About 28 lakh cyber-fraud cases reported in 2025; Rs 52,976 crore lost in 6 years (MHA data). — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/india/citizens-lost-over-52976-crore-to-cyber-frauds-in-6-years-mha-data-reveals) [U]
- Hyderabad: seniors lost Rs 102.02 crore in 403 cases, Jan 2025 to Jul 2026 (average about Rs 25 lakh per case); digital arrest was second, 69 cases and Rs 31.93 crore. — [Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/hyderabad-cybercrime-senior-citizens-lose-rs-102-crore-in-19-months) [U]
- Individual cases: 92-year-old Noida professor lost about Rs 1.02 crore (Sept 2025); elderly NRI couple in Delhi about Rs 14.84 crore (Jan 2026). — [Business Standard / press](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/digital-arrest-scam-fear-educated-elderly-victim-users-fall-cyber-fraud-126062500485_1.html) [U]
- NCRB Crime in India 2023: 27,886 crimes against seniors, down 2.3% from 28,545 in 2022. Madhya Pradesh 5,738, Maharashtra 5,115, Tamil Nadu 2,104, Karnataka 1,840. Simple hurt 27.3%. Pendency 44.4%. Delhi 1,361 cases among metros. — [NewsDrum (PTI)](https://www.newsdrum.in/national/crimes-against-senior-citizens-dip-to-27886-in-2023-maharashtra-mp-tn-report-high-numbers-ncrb-10516723)

### Inferences
- Rs 4,005 crore over 1.03 lakh complaints is about Rs 3.9 lakh per complaint on average (my division). Average loss per case is large, and a family would pay to prevent a single event.
- NCRP complaints undercount real incidence; underreporting among seniors is widely asserted but I found no measured rate.

### Gaps
- Share of all NCRP victims who are 60+: not found. Digital-arrest national numbers for seniors: not found. RBI annual-report fraud figures not retrieved. I4C primary publications not checked.

## 6. Elder abuse, mobility, hearing and vision, paperwork, digital exclusion

### Takeaway
Abuse is reported by roughly 16-19% in HelpAge surveys, with under-reporting. Vision problems are very common; hearing impairment less so on self-report. Paperwork and digital exclusion have no hard numbers in what I retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- HelpAge India 2014: 19.4% of respondents faced some abuse; verbal most common, then neglect, disrespect, emotional, economic, physical. — [PARI](https://ruralindiaonline.org/en/library/resource/state-of-elderly-in-india-2014/)
- HelpAge women's report (7,911 older women): about 16% reported abuse; only about 16% of those reported it; sons most named, then other relatives and daughters-in-law. Year reported as 2023 by the PDF name and 2025 by news; unreconciled. — [HelpAge PDF](https://www.helpageindia.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Women-Ageing-Invisible-or-Empowered-a-HelpAge-India-2023-report.pdf); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/analysis/national-report-over-50-elderly-women-say-abuse-prevalent-in-india) [U]
- A 2024 HelpAge survey of 5,000+ elders in 20 cities: 65% feel financially insecure (single secondary source). — [The Wire](https://thewire.in/rights/around-82-of-elders-live-with-family-where-most-abuse-happens-indias-invisible-crisis) [U]
- HelpAge 2024 "Ageing in India: Exploring Preparedness and Response to Care Challenges" contains abuse and digital-access sections; I could not read the figures. — [HelpAge PDF](https://helpageindia.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/REPORT-Ageing-in-India-Exploring-Preparedness-Response-to-Care-Challenges-A-HelpAge-India-Report.pdf)
- Sensory, LASI wave 1, 45+, self-reported: vision impairment 48.0%, hearing impairment 6.9%, dual about 5.0%. Cataract (50+): 14.85%, surgical coverage 76.95%. — [Frontiers Public Health 2024](https://frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2024.1424031/pdf); [Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-38229-z.pdf)

### Inferences
- Abuse is mostly by family, who are also the customers, which complicates any product that reports to children.

### Gaps
- Not found: pension life certificate failure rates, insurance claim rejection among seniors, bank KYC friction, smartphone and internet use among 60+, transport and mobility data.

## 7. Caregiver side and willingness to pay

### Takeaway
Real prices exist for paid care, but I found no rigorous willingness-to-pay survey. Spending evidence comes from care-provider blogs. Most elder needs are met by unpaid family labour.

### Cited Findings
- Price ranges per month (vendor blogs [U]): part-time attendant Rs 12,000-25,000; live-in Rs 15,000-50,000; home nursing Rs 20,000-50,000; 24/7 nursing Rs 70,000-1,20,000. Other sources give Rs 10,000-35,000 and Rs 35,000-50,000. They disagree. — [Samarth](https://care.samarth.community/blog/long-distance-care-giving/24x7-in-home-care-cost-india/); [WIS](https://wis.it.com/how-much-does-a-carer-cost-in-india)
- Dementia household cost per year (see section 4).
- Hip fracture and chronic disease costs are largely out of pocket (sections 2-3).
- Policy commentary: home elder care is mostly family and household work, with little public provision. — [Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1091264/indias-elder-care-was-supposed-to-reach-homes-but-households-and-families-are-taking-on-the-work); [Newslaundry, 2026-03-09](https://www.newslaundry.com/2026/03/09/elder-care-was-meant-to-reach-homes-in-most-of-india-it-hasnt)
- NRI survey [U]: 43 of 100 NRIs say visa uncertainty impedes caregiving. Barriers only, no spend. — [Samarth](https://care.samarth.community/?p=135044)

### Inferences: ranked evidence table (my judgement; "pay evidence" is the weakest column)

| Rank | Problem | How common | How severe | Pay evidence | Who pays |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cyber fraud and scams | 1.03 lakh senior complaints in 2025 (undercount) | Rs 4,005 crore lost; Rs 3.9 lakh avg per complaint (my division) | Indirect: losses are large; no purchase data | Family, after the loss |
| 2 | Chronic disease unmanaged, non-adherence | 31.7-38.9% treated among hypertensives 45+; adherence 24-73% by site | Stroke, cardiac events (not quantified here) | Medicines are already bought; reminders not priced | Elder, family |
| 3 | Dementia | 7.4-8.4% of 60+ | Very high; caregivers 40-72% distressed | Rs 29,000-3 lakh per household per year | Family |
| 4 | Falls and hip fracture | 5-6% fall injury per 2 years; 19% one-year hip fracture mortality | High | Out of pocket for surgery | Family |
| 5 | Loneliness and depression | 13-15% lonely; 8-9% depressed (mostly undiagnosed) | Moderate to high | None found | Mostly unpaid need |
| 6 | Abuse and neglect | 16-19% in HelpAge surveys; 16% report it | High | None | Unpaid |
| 7 | Sensory loss | Vision 48%, hearing 6.9% (45+, self-reported) | Moderate | Not assessed | Elder |
| 8 | Paperwork and digital exclusion | no data retrieved | unknown | unknown | unknown |

### Gaps
- No survey on willingness to pay by adult children, NRI or domestic. No attendant quality and trust data. No sandwich-generation time-use data. These need primary research, for example founder interviews.
