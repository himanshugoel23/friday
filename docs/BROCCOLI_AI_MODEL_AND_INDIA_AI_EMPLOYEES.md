### A8. Go-to-market
1. Sell to the **owner's labour budget** (CSR salaries, missed-call revenue), not to a software budget. Vizologi's framing: https://vizologi.com/vertical-ai-is-eating-the-front-desk-the-business-model-behind-ai-receptionists/ (fetched 2026-11-14; it cites Bessemer: LLM-native vertical firms capture about 80% of traditional vertical software contract value, about 400% growth, about 65% gross margin; and BLS receptionist median wage $38,010).
2. Ride the incumbent's marketplace (ServiceTitan), so the buyer is already in the ecosystem and trusts the integration.
3. Sell to franchise and multi-location brands (Neighborly, Authority Brands, Mister Sparky): one deal, many sites. "Pilot, prove ROI, launch, roll out to the brand, expand to every location" is their own described path.
4. Hire people from the trade (a plumber as customer-success lead) so the playbooks sound right.
5. Content: named AI "employees" with a face, an "EBITDA lever" pitch to owners and private-equity-backed roll-ups.

### A9. Onboarding and human-in-the-loop
- Onboarding: dedicated implementation team and an "AI Strategy Lead", phased pilot to full roll-out (own site). Not self-serve. Open roles include Head of AI Success and Account Executive (YC page). So it is a **high-touch, services-heavy** onboarding.
- Humans: no evidence of a human answering desk. Smith.ai's page says Broccoli "relies solely on your internal staff for human transfers" (rival claim). The "QA and Coaching" product assumes the customer still has human CSRs. Human-in-the-loop = the customer's own staff, plus Broccoli's own AI QA.

### A10. Moats and risks
Moats: (a) deep integration into dispatch software, (b) trade playbooks (emergency vs routine, membership, estimate follow-up), (c) many agents sharing the customer's job history, (d) enterprise trust (SOC 2), (e) switching cost once staff work around it (Vizologi).
Risks: voice quality is commoditising; incumbents ship built-ins (ServiceTitan "AI Virtual Agent", Housecall Pro CSR AI, Jobber Receptionist at $29/month for 30 conversations then $0.79 each, per NextPhone page); pricing is opaque and 6-month terms may drive churn [U]; one wrong emergency routing can end an account.

### A12. The lesson in one paragraph
Broccoli is not "a voice bot". It is **a team of named AI employees, wired into the owner's job software, sold on the owner's labour budget, set up by humans**. The money is in the chain: answer, book, remind, follow up the estimate, renew membership, ask for a review. Voice alone is cheap and getting cheaper (US self-serve from $29 to $200 a month); the integrated team is what is sold at about $1,000 or more a month [U]. Copy the structure, not the US price.

## Part C. A team of AI employees for one Indian business

### C1. The team (names are placeholders; give the owner one friendly name per job)
Channel key: C = phone call, W = WhatsApp. "Human" = the owner or their staff, or the Friday desk (C5).

| # | Employee | Trigger | Channel | Data it needs | Hands off to | Guardrails | Owner sees (metric) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Receptionist | missed, declined or after-hours call (forwarded) | C in, then W | business facts, open slots, price list | Booking (wants a slot), Support (a question), Human (urgent, angry, medical emergency) | never quotes a price not in the list, never gives medical advice, truthful if asked "are you AI", 90-second cap (exists) | calls caught, % turned into a lead |
| 2 | Booking | any lead wanting a slot | W (C if caller is on the line) | calendar, service durations, doctor/stylist | Reminder (after booking), Payment (if advance needed), Human (clash) | confirms every booking by reading it back; never double-books; changes need caller confirmation | bookings made, slots filled |

### C2. One customer record (shared memory)
One row per customer, keyed by phone number, owned by the business (not by an agent):
- who: name, phone, language, WhatsApp opted-in or not, "do not contact" flag;
- history: every call (summary only, no audio), message, booking, visit, payment, review;
- state: lead stage (new, asked, booked, visited, lapsed), next planned touch, who owns it now (an agent or a human);
- rules: the business's own facts (price list, hours, doctor rules, cancellation terms) in one editable sheet.
Only one agent may "own" a customer at a time. That prevents the classic failure: three agents texting the same person.

### C3. Hand-off graph
```
 caller -> [Receptionist] --wants slot--> [Booking] --booked--> [Reminder] --visit done--> [Review]
                |  \--question--> [Support]                         |                        |
                |   \--big enquiry--> [Sales] --> owner closes       no-show                  unhappy
                |                                                    v                        v
                \--urgent/angry/medical--> HUMAN <---complaint--- [Follow-up/Reactivation]   HUMAN (owner)
 [Payment] <-- booking has advance / invoice due        all agents --> shared customer record --> [Manager] --> owner's 8 am WhatsApp
```
Rules: (1) an agent hands off with a one-line reason and the record, never a raw transcript; (2) any anger, medical emergency, legal word, refund demand or "talk to a person" goes to a human at once; (3) a handed-off customer is frozen: no agent messages them until the human marks it done; (4) every agent's last action is written to the record.

### C4. The owner's 8 am WhatsApp briefing (the product the owner actually sees)
```
Good morning Dr. Mehta. Yesterday: 14 calls caught while you were busy, 9 bookings, 2 no-shows (both reminded twice).
Today: 11 appointments, 2 free slots at 4 and 5 pm (I am offering them to the waiting list).
Needs you: (1) Mrs Rao asks about braces cost, bigger enquiry. [Call her] [Reply for me]
          (2) Mr Khan unhappy about billing. [Call] 
Money: Rs 18,000 booked yesterday from calls you would have missed. Rs 6,500 reminders due.
```
Buttons are the same pattern as the existing receptionist summary. A weekly "money found" message is the renewal argument.

### C5. Human fallback desk
Reuse the Robin desk design (docs/ROBIN_CONCIERGE_DESK.md): AI first, a human joins, with a one-screen hand-over card, an audit log, and a second-person review of every incident. For businesses, add a business-hours line so that when an agent says "let me get someone", a person on the desk (or the owner on their phone) takes the call within minutes. In the first pilot the founder is the desk. Do not promise "24/7" until staffed.

### C7. Build order (four stages)
1. **MVP (weeks 1 to 4), one vertical, WhatsApp-first:** Receptionist (exists) + Booking + Follow-up, one shared customer record, the 8 am briefing, founder as human desk. Booking into a Google Calendar or a plain sheet. Real WhatsApp onboarding must be fixed first.
2. **Stage 2 (weeks 5 to 8): Reminder / No-show + Review.** These are cheap (templates) and show the clearest numbers.
3. **Stage 3 (weeks 9 to 14): Payment reminders + Support/FAQ + Sales qualifier**, plus Hindi voice call-backs for opted-in customers (after a TRAI legal read).
4. **Stage 4: second vertical** by copying the facts sheet and playbook, and a small human desk. Only now consider integrations (a clinic software, Tally, a booking tool).

|---|---|---|
| Setup | free or Rs 1,999 (done by you in under a day) | Edesy charges Rs 50,000 setup, which small owners resist; free removes the barrier |
| Platform fee | Rs 3,999 to Rs 6,999 per month per location | same band as Edesy/CallMissed (Rs 4,000 to Rs 5,000) but includes the whole team |
| Per outcome | Rs 50 to Rs 150 per **confirmed new** booking (owner confirms "showed up" in the briefing) | ties price to results; Broccoli-style opaque contracts are the opposite of what a small Indian owner trusts |
| Cap | fee + outcome never above Rs 12,000/month for a small clinic | no bill shock |
| Contract | monthly, cancel by one WhatsApp message | the first 10 customers must feel zero lock-in |
Anchor to the owner's payroll: a receptionist costs about Rs 19,621 (salon) to Rs 38,642 (Bengaluru clinic) a month (CallMissed page, Indeed/Salary.com, undated) [U]. Selling at about a quarter of that is easy to explain.

### C9. Unit economics (hypotheses; arithmetic on invented inputs)
One small clinic, one month: 250 inbound calls, of which 40% reach Friday as missed or after hours = 100 calls at 1.5 minutes = 150 minutes. Voice cost at Rs 2.5 to Rs 3 per minute (HANDOFF section 3 cost note: TTS + STT + Vobiz, Sarvam list prices) is about Rs 375 to Rs 450. WhatsApp: about 600 template messages at about Rs 0.115 (utility) to Rs 0.86 (marketing) is about Rs 70 to Rs 520. LLM cost: small, [U]. So direct cost is roughly Rs 600 to Rs 1,200 a month, versus a fee of Rs 4,000 to Rs 7,000 plus outcomes: gross margin of about 75% to 85% before human time and support. The real cost is **your time**: if setup is 1 day and support is 2 hours a month per customer, 10 customers is a full-time job. The desk hours, not the AI, set the margin. If the AI books 15 extra visits a month at Rs 1,500, that is Rs 22,500 of revenue for the clinic against Rs 6,000 paid: an easy "yes" if it is true; the test is whether it is true.

### C10. Onboarding in one day (target)
| Hour | Step |
|---|---|
| 0 to 1 | 30-minute call or WhatsApp voice note: owner fills the facts sheet (services, prices, hours, doctors, FAQs, what is urgent) |
| 1 to 2 | you load it, set the greeting and the owner's name, approve the Hinglish lines (hear-before-change applies) |
| 2 to 3 | owner dials the forwarding codes (already generated), does the test call, checks carrier voicemail is off |
| 3 to 4 | WhatsApp: owner saves the Friday number, approves the templates (reminder, review, recall); import last 100 customers from their phone or sheet with a consent note |
| Day 1 evening | first briefing sent; day 2 morning real briefing |
Blockers today: per-business WhatsApp number and templates, the unverified forwarding probe, DPDP notice text.

### C11. Two-week validation plan (best vertical: dental and skin clinics; run salons in parallel as the cheap sibling)
Rules: no real call to a business without the founder's approval (CLAUDE.md rule 5). Interviews are by you, in person or by WhatsApp.

| Days | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 to 3 | List 30 clinic owners/managers you can reach by referral in one city. Offer a 20-minute chat. No building |
| 3 to 7 | Ask: how many calls a day go unanswered (ask them to check missed calls on their phone, showing you the log)? what is a new patient worth? how many no-shows a week? who answers after 7 pm? what do you pay your receptionist? have you tried Edesy, CallMissed, WhatsApp Business AI, or a human service, and why not? what price would feel like "yes, obviously" and "too much"? |
| 7 to 10 | Offer a free 14-day pilot: they forward missed calls; you (or the founder desk) run the receptionist + booking + follow-up with a human behind the scenes where the AI is not ready ("concierge" style, honest about it) |
| 10 to 14 | Count: missed calls caught, bookings, no-shows reduced, owner replies to the 8 am briefing; then ask for money at a price you named on day 3 |

**Pass line (by day 14):** at least 5 of 30 contacted owners agree to a pilot; at least 3 pilots run a full week and the owner reads the briefing on most days; at least 2 say yes to a paid month at Rs 4,000 or more; at least 10 extra bookings per clinic that the owner confirms were caught by Friday; **zero** serious incidents (wrong urgent routing, leaked patient data).
**Fail line:** fewer than 3 of 30 accept a pilot, or nobody will forward calls, or owners say "Meta's WhatsApp AI / Edesy is enough", or the clinics' real pain is something else (then switch vertical to salons or auto service).

## Part D. Honest risks

1. **Crowded.** Verified: CallMissed, Edesy, Pype, Jio Haptik, Bolna and Ringg (as builders' platforms), plus Meta's own free-looking Business AI inside WhatsApp Business. Anyone can assemble a receptionist from Bolna or Vapi in a weekend. A single voice receptionist is a commodity at Rs 4,000 to Rs 5,000 a month.
2. **Why customers churn.** (a) the AI misses or mishandles one important patient, (b) the owner cannot see money, so the fee looks like a cost, (c) staff dislike it (it replaces or watches them), (d) reminders annoy customers, (e) setup breaks (carrier voicemail answers first; forwarding needs re-dialling after a SIM or plan change), (f) the owner returns to free Meta Business AI, (g) seasonal verticals pause (coaching). The weekly "money found" message is the main defence.
3. **TRAI.** Inbound pickup is safest; any outbound calling, reactivation or promotions can fall under telemarketing and consent rules, and the AI-specific disclosure text is not confirmed. Mitigation: WhatsApp for follow-ups, calls only to opted-in existing customers, legal read first.
4. **WhatsApp / Meta dependence.** Meta can change templates, prices (rates changed 2026-04-01 and again for service messages from 2026-10-01 per a reseller blog), eligibility or bot rules, and Meta now competes with its own Business AI. Account bans for spammy reminders would stop the business. Keep opt-in records, low volume per customer, and a second channel (SMS or calls).
5. **Vobiz / telephony dependence.** One carrier partner for all calls; forwarding behaviour on Jio, Airtel, Vi and BSNL is **unverified** (RECEPTIONIST.md section 11); whether Vobiz passes the redirecting number is unknown, so "which business was this call for" is not yet solved. Mitigation: a dedicated number per business (extra cost) or a second provider.
6. **Hindi voice quality.** No line has been heard on a real call yet; Hinglish STT on 8 kHz forwarded calls, names, noisy shops and clinic accents are unproven. One bad call in front of a patient costs trust. Mitigation: short fixed lines, read-backs, text fallback on WhatsApp, and the human desk.
7. **Health data and liability.** A clinic's patient list is sensitive under DPDP; a wrong "not urgent" on a medical call is a safety event. Keep the existing "when unsure, flag it" rule; no advice; the owner is the fiduciary.
8. **Solo-founder capacity.** Ten pilots means ten different facts sheets, calendars and phone setups. If every customer needs hand-holding, you are an agency, not software.
9. **What would make this fail.** (a) owners will not forward their calls (trust), (b) real missed-call volume is too low to show value (Edesy itself says under 50 calls a month is not worth it), (c) the bookings the AI "finds" are not confirmed by the owner, (d) Meta's free AI is "good enough" for chat and clinics only care about calls, (e) one serious incident, (f) you try to build all nine employees before one customer pays.
10. **Evidence gaps in this document.** Broccoli's price, churn and margins are unknown; Indian willingness to pay beyond Rs 5,000 a month is untested; I did not verify Vodex, Smallest.ai, Yellow.ai, Zoho, Interakt, WATI, AiSensy, Urban Company or JustDial; legal points are summaries, not legal advice.

## Recommended single next action
Do the two-week clinic and salon interviews (Part C11) before building anything new. In parallel, fix the two real blockers that every vertical shares: real WhatsApp onboarding with approved templates, and the forwarding probe on your own SIM.
