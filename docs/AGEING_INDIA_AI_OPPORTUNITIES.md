# India's ageing boom is real; paying customers are unproven

**Short answer: the need is huge and growing, but the proof that families will pay for AI products is thin, so this is a promising niche to test, not yet a strong business.** India has roughly 150-167 million people aged 60+ today, heading to about 227 million (15%) by 2036 and about 347 million (20.8%) by 2050 ([UNFPA India](https://india.unfpa.org/en/news/india-ageing-elderly-make-20-population-2050-unfpa-report); [JLL](https://www.jll.com/en-in/insights/india-silver-economy-from-niche-to-necessity)). The pain is measurable: senior citizens lost Rs 4,005 crore to cyber fraud in 2025 alone, and India has trained fewer than 40,000 caregivers against a need of about 2 million ([Asianet Newsable](https://newsable.asianetnews.com/india/senior-citizens-women-lost-rs-7-769-crore-to-cyber-fraud-in-2025-articleshow-u9oad8w) [U]; [The Ken](https://the-ken.com/story/india-needs-about-2-million-caregivers-the-govt-has-trained-fewer-than-40000/)). But the notes found no survey of what adult children will pay, the funded incumbents still lose money (Emoha lost about Rs 36.7 crore on Rs 74 crore revenue in FY25), and a funded senior app (GenWise) shut down in 2025 ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/exclusive/exclusive-eldercare-platform-emoha-reports-rs-7435-cr-revenue-in-fy25-cuts-losses-by-32-9986550); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/exclusive-kunal-shah-backed-genwise-shuts-down-operations)). Abroad, the models that lasted were paid by a city, a bank or an insurer, not by families. My advice: do not build hardware. Sell Robin as a phone-call service to the adult child (especially NRIs and metro children), test a scam shield, and try to land one institutional payer. Each idea below has a 2-week test with a pass/fail line. If the first two tests fail, stop.

How to read this report. Every claim not checked at a source is marked **[U]** (unverified). The research notes had real gaps, and I list them rather than guess. All prices and thresholds in section 7 are my hypotheses, not findings.

## 1. Demographics and timing: a slow wave that is already here

**The count.** Sources differ because of base year and definition. UNFPA says 149 million aged 60+ in 2022; JLL says 166.9 million now. A safe planning range is **150-165 million in 2026, about 190 million in 2030, about 225 million in 2036, about 345 million in 2050** (the 2030 figure is from [JLL](https://www.jll.com/en-in/insights/india-silver-economy-from-niche-to-necessity); 2036 and 2050 from [UNFPA](https://india.unfpa.org/en/news/india-ageing-elderly-make-20-population-2050-unfpa-report)). The 60+ group grows several times faster than the population as a whole (about 134% growth for 60+ from 2022 to 2050 against 18% overall, per [Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/amp/india-news/elderly-population-in-india-likely-to-double-by-2050-unfpa-india-chief-124072100120_1.html), snippet only). The 80+ group is projected to grow about 279% ([UNFPA](https://india.unfpa.org/en/news/india-ageing-elderly-make-20-population-2050-unfpa-report)). The south, Himachal and Punjab age first. Over 19,500 Indians turn 60 every day ([The Ken](https://the-ken.com/story/india-needs-about-2-million-caregivers-the-govt-has-trained-fewer-than-40000/)).

**Living alone.** The latest national data is old: LASI wave 1, fieldwork 2017-18. Among 60+, **5.7% live alone and 20.3% live with a spouse only; 36.2% are widowed; about 68% live with children (40.6% with spouse and children, 27.6% with children and others)** ([LASI fact sheet](https://www.iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/other_files/LASI_W1_Factsheet_INDIA.pdf)). Adding alone and spouse-only gives about 26%, or roughly 40 million people (my arithmetic). UNFPA's summary says "about one-fifth", so the exact split is unclear. Widowed elders living alone were 56% more likely to be depressed ([PMC8568934](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8568934), snippet). This matters for sales: most elders live with family, so the buyer is usually someone else, and the "alone" segment, while a minority, is large in absolute terms and growing.

**Money.** Pensions are thin. Only 7.7% of 60+ received a retirement pension in 2017-18; mean household pension income was Rs 3,604 a year; per-capita household income was Rs 44,726 a year (urban Rs 65,608) ([LASI fact sheet](https://www.iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/other_files/LASI_W1_Factsheet_INDIA.pdf)). HelpAge (2024) found only 29% have access to any social security, one in three earned nothing in the past year, and 65% feel financially insecure ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/only-29-elderly-have-access-to-social-security-schemes-like-pension-pf-study/2131998), snippet). Health cover for 70+ is public: Ayushman Vay Vandana gives Rs 5 lakh per family a year to all 70+, with about 1.36 crore cards issued by September 2026 against an eligible pool of about 6 crore [U on the denominator] ([Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/amp/news/health/hospital-diagnostics/36229-hospitals-empanelled-435-crore-cards-issued-under-ab-pmjay-166744)). The only sourced "affluent" count is JLL's **about 1.7 million urban, financially independent senior households in 2026, rising to 2.1 million by 2030**. That is about 1% of seniors. The senior is rarely the buyer.

**Phones.** NSS 2025 data show smartphone access of **44.7% for ages 60-69 and 42% for 70+**; more than half rely wholly or partly on basic phones; about 9 in 10 older internet users go online daily; online banking ability is 53% of 60+; and only 8% of 60+ can report cybercrime on the national portal ([Ideas for India](https://www.ideasforindia.in/topics/governance/making-indias-digital-public-infrastructure-work-for-older-citizens)). HelpAge's urban-only survey found 40% own a smartphone and 66% find digital tools too confusing ([Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/news/digital-divide-among-elderly-66-find-digital-tools-too-confusing)). Reading this together: roughly 60-70 million 60+ can be reached by smartphone and the rest only by an ordinary voice call (my inference). **Voice-call first is the right channel.** WhatsApp is widely believed to be the top app among seniors, but no national WhatsApp, UPI, voice-versus-typing or language-preference figure was found. Treat those as unknown.

**Not in the notes (gaps).** No count of NRI-family or metro-child households; no urban/rural split of 60+; no LASI wave 2; no pension numbers from PFRDA or EPFO; no dependable years-to-double comparison with Japan, China and Korea. The common claim that India ages as fast as those countries but at far lower income is plausible but **[U]**. Market-size claims range from USD 10-15 billion (senior care today, [The Ken](https://the-ken.com/story/india-needs-about-2-million-caregivers-the-govt-has-trained-fewer-than-40000/)) to USD 50 billion by 2030 ([Realty n More](https://realtynmore.com/indias-silver-economy-a-50-billion-market-opportunity-by-2030/) [U], method unknown). Do not quote any single "silver economy" number.

The notes judge willingness to pay (WTP) as the weakest column. **No study asked Indian adult children or seniors what they would pay.** The only price evidence is vendor price lists and blogs. Ranking below is the notes' judgement, lightly adjusted by me.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | Unmanaged chronic disease, missed medicines | 31.7-38.9% of hypertensives 45+ on treatment or controlled; adherence 24-73% by site | Stroke, heart events (not quantified in notes) | Medicines are bought already; reminders have no price evidence | Senior, family |

Three readings matter. First, **fraud is the best-measured and the most "buyable" problem**, because one event costs lakhs and a child can imagine it. Hyderabad alone recorded Rs 102 crore lost by seniors in 403 cases in 19 months, with digital arrest the second most common type ([Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/hyderabad-cybercrime-senior-citizens-lose-rs-102-crore-in-19-months) [U]). Second, forgetfulness is a top self-reported cause of missed medicines (67.5% in one rural West Bengal study), and a phone call can fix forgetfulness, but not cost or poor pharmacy supply (72.3% and 66.3% in the same study) ([Natl J Community Med](https://doaj.org/article/36809bc322974bda88bdd7e80765a08a)). Third, half of diabetes and 64% of hypertension in 45+ is undiagnosed ([BMC Health Serv Res](https://bmchealthservres.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12913-023-09318-6.pdf)), so screening, not reminders, is the first gap.

**Gaps.** No data on pension life-certificate failures, insurance claim rejections, bank KYC friction, transport, paperwork, time-to-discovery after a fall, or national medicine adherence for 60+. Falls are two-year recall, not yearly. The estimates for falls range from about 10% to 23% depending on definition.

## 3. What other countries built: the payer decided who survived

**Japan.** The state spent well over USD 300 million on care-robot R&D by 2018, yet a 2019 survey of 9,000+ institutions found about 10% had adopted any care robot, and only 2% of home-care providers had tried one ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2023/01/09/1065135/japan-automating-eldercare-robots)). Robear was never used, the Hug lifter was dropped within days, Pepper needed a staff member beside it and is discontinued, and one resident became so attached to Paro that she refused meals without it. The evidence is contested: a study of about 860 nursing homes with 50% subsidies linked robot adoption to higher staff numbers, not fewer ([Stanford summary](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2026/08/care-robots-japan-employment-labor-shortage-research), page blocked; [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28322.pdf)). Both can be true: robots added work and kept staff. Simple monitoring sensors were the most adopted category (14.9% of homes in 2017). The cheap model was **mimamori watch-over**: about 1,000 yen a month, roughly Rs 600 [U on exchange rate]. Japan Post's version was drastically downsized by 2017, and Yokohama subsidises approved services by up to 1,000 yen a month ([BabyTech](https://babytech.jp/en/2017/07/japanpost_jp_withdrawal_from_senior_care_business/); [Yokohama](https://en.city.yokohama.lg.jp/kenko-iryo-fukushi/fukushi-kaigo/koreisha-kaigo/kaigohoken-igai-service/zaitaku-yoengo-shien/mimamori.html)). In 2025, **76,941 people died alone at home and about 22,222 were found 8+ days later** ([Nippon.com](https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-data/h02768/)). India has no equivalent count.

**South Korea.** This is the closest match to Robin. Naver CareCall phones seniors on a schedule, with social workers reviewing alerts; it was in 128 local districts by August 2024 ([Asia Economy](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2024090509175645982)). It flagged "hundreds of emergencies", for example a 77-year-old with an acute hernia who got surgery within hours ([NYT via The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/05/02/they-said-ai-saved-me-how-south-korea-is-checking-on-its-seniors)). Failures are instructive: it cut people off, a loud TV confused it, it promised rice it could not send, and it missed figures of speech. In a rural pilot of 2,990 people, **11.2% never answered a call in three months, and the least likely to answer were poorer, more disabled and lived with others** ([Gerontechnology](https://journal.gerontechnology.org/currentIssueContent.aspx?aid=4870)). SK Telecom's NUGU speaker was mostly used for music (95.1%) and only 64.4% used emotional conversation ([Asiae](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2020052009385955911)). The payer was always a city. Prices were not disclosed, and no independent trial exists. Hyodol dolls show a 24.7-35.7% fall in high-risk loneliness or depression, but the maker ran the study ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10694515)).

**China.** The state treats the silver economy as a pillar and is building long-term-care insurance, with enrolment reported at 320 million in July 2026, up from 190 million, a jump to treat with care ([gov.cn](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202607/09/content_WS6a4f9fa8c6d00ca5f9a0c18f.html)). A Tsinghua report says unclear device classification slows scaling ([Tsinghua](https://ccirm.sem.tsinghua.edu.cn/uploads/master/file/20260617/6a321eea025f5.pdf)). Basic 4G SOS watches wholesale at about USD 27-33 ([Alibaba](https://electronics.alibaba.com/product/elderly-emergency-bell)); Chinese retail prices and adoption of AI companions were not verified.

**United States.** The one companion with a government payer is ElliQ: New York's aging agency signed a USD 2.1 million, three-year deal in 2026 ([Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/nysofa-expands-partnership-intuition-robotics-bring-elliq-more-seniors/)). Its "94-95% less lonely" figures are self-reported, with no control group. Consumer price is a USD 249.99 enrolment fee plus a monthly fee ([ElliQ FAQ](https://elliq.com/pages/faqs)). **Amazon's USD 19.99/month Alexa Together launched in December 2021 and was withdrawn in May 2025**, no reason given ([Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/md/news/devices/alexa-together-launches-to-help-customers-remotely-care-for-loved-ones)). Money flows to B2B: Sensi.ai (audio monitoring sold to home-care agencies) raised a USD 45 million Series C in 2025 and SafelyYou a USD 43 million Series C ([Newswire](https://www.newswire.com/news/sensi-ai-raises-45m-series-c-extending-its-dominance-of-care-22653210); [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/safelyyou-secures-43m-ai-assisted-senior-living-and-more-funding-news?leadId=1491391) [U on date]). Papa sold companionship to Medicare Advantage plans, then faced a Senate letter over safety complaints and shrinking plan benefits ([Senate Aging Committee](https://www.aging.senate.gov/press-releases/casey-urges-medicare-agency-to-prevent-abuse-of-older-adults-and-workers-at-caregiving-tech-company); [Becker's](https://www.beckerspayer.com/payer/medicare-advantage/insurers-pull-medicare-advantage-plans-as-profit-pressures-mount/)). Scam protection is sold two ways: Carefull at USD 29.99 a month direct, and EverSafe through banks, with bank sponsorship ([Q2 marketplace](https://q2developer.com/marketplace/apps/carefull-senior--family-protections/marketing) [U]; [EverSafe](https://www.eversafe.com/banks/)). Investors at an April 2025 panel doubted direct-to-consumer AgeTech, said barriers are low because everyone builds on the same AI models, and asked for "hard dollar ROI" ([MedCity](https://medcitynews.com/2025/04/agetech-venture-capital-healthcare-medicare/)).

**Europe.** UK telecare is paid by councils and housing providers and is stressed by the analogue-to-digital switchover; about 20% of calls from analogue equipment fail on digital lines (2024 figure) ([Housing LIN](https://www.housinglin.org.uk/blogs/TAPPI-Days-A-Telecare-National-Plan-for-Action/)). Cera, a digital-first home-care provider, raised about USD 150 million in January 2025 ([Digital Health](https://www.digitalhealth.net/2025/01/cera-secures-more-than-123m-to-roll-out-ai-led-home-healthcare)). Nordic welfare tech and Nobi were not researched.

**What it means.**

| Worked (cheaply) | Failed or stalled |
|---|---|
| Phone check-ins with human escalation (Korea) | Humanoid and lifting robots that need staff time (Japan) |
| Passive sensors, monitoring | Consumer-paid remote-caregiver add-on on a mass platform (Alexa Together) |
| About 1,000 yen/month watch-over, city-subsidised | Sole-vendor dependence; thin margins at the market leader (Japan Post) |
| B2B tools that cut agency labour (Sensi.ai) | Plan-funded benefits that vanish when plans cut (Papa) |

**Not researched:** Singapore, Taiwan, pill dispensers, care-coordination firms, Nordic reimbursement, hardware cost of goods, and certification costs.

## 5. Hardware or software: for a solo founder, software plus people

No bill-of-materials, certification, returns or support-cost data was found in the notes, so this is reasoning from indirect evidence. What the evidence does say: entry-level 4G SOS watches are available at about USD 30 wholesale ([Alibaba](https://electronics.alibaba.com/product/elderly-emergency-bell)) and sell in India at roughly Rs 9,000-17,000 [U]. That price is above a month of most family budgets. Wrist-worn fall detection has low accuracy and false alarms lead people to stop wearing it ([McKnight's](https://www.mcknightsseniorliving.com/?p=8037) [U]). Japan's robot history shows that devices which need staff time stay in drawers. The notes also flag that a device making health claims may fall under medical-device rules, and nothing in the sources puts an alert-only fall watch in a class, so a legal opinion is needed first (see section 8). Compare that with what Robin already is: a phone call needs no device, no inventory and no shipping, and CareCall's data suggest smartphone owners answer less often than feature-phone users, which favours calls.

| | Hardware (SOS/fall watch) | Software + phone calls (Robin) | Human service (concierge) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upfront cash | Inventory, returns, certification [U] | Calling and AI costs only | Staff salaries |
| Support burden | Charging, wear, false alarms, repair (Hyodol downtime) | Missed calls, wrong replies | Highest |
| Regulation | Device status, radio and safety approvals [U] | Calling rules, data law | Licences if medical |
| Margin | Device is a commodity; value is the response service | Highest if the human share stays low | Lowest; Emoha shows the cost |
| Fit for 1 person + 2-3 part-timers | Poor | Best | Only as paid add-on |

**Verdict on hardware:** resell a ready-made watch only as a paid add-on after the call service proves demand. Do not design, certify or stock devices.

## 6. The workforce shortage: a real tailwind, but it favours helping families, not replacing carers

The Ken (October 2026) reports a need for about 2 million caregivers against fewer than 40,000 government-trained, and says firms offering hospital-level pay, food and housing still cannot hire enough ([The Ken](https://the-ken.com/story/india-needs-about-2-million-caregivers-the-govt-has-trained-fewer-than-40000/)). Other press says 4.3 million are needed against 36,000 available, and a 2025 conference cited 5,000-6,000 trained ([Devdiscourse](https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/headlines/3526055-bridging-the-care-gap-indias-urgent-need-for-professional-elderly-caregivers) [U]). The figures conflict and methods are unknown, so use "very large and unmeasured". There are no minimum training standards for geriatric dementia care ([PMC11712114](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11712114/)). One anecdote: a Bengaluru live-in caregiver earns Rs 24,000 a month, of which the agency keeps Rs 5,000 ([The Ken](https://the-ken.com/story/india-needs-about-2-million-caregivers-the-govt-has-trained-fewer-than-40000/)). Human 24x7 care costs Rs 70,000-1,20,000 a month at the high end (vendor blogs, which disagree: [Samarth](https://care.samarth.community/blog/long-distance-care-giving/24x7-in-home-care-cost-india/)).

Two honest conclusions follow. One: the shortage makes **tools that stretch scarce carers and families** attractive: check-ins, reminders, shift reports, escalation. That fits the Korean and US B2B lesson (Sensi.ai sells to agencies). Two: Japan shows tech does not remove the labour; it shifts it. Robin should plan a human desk of 2-3 part-timers from day one, and treat their hours as the cost that decides margins. A shortage of carers also means your own concierge and emergency staff are hard to hire and retain, which is a risk to name, not a given.

## 7. Loneliness and AI companions: a weaker business than it sounds, with real risks

**What the evidence says.** The loneliness research is large but low quality. The main JAMA Network Open review (70 studies, 8,259 people) rated certainty "very low" ([Hoang 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9577679/)). Multicomponent community programmes had a pooled effect of -0.67 (a moderate drop), but **technology-only interventions in the community had a small, non-significant effect (-0.19, CI -0.51 to 0.14)**, and group, human-led formats did better. Social robots show a pooled effect of -0.59 across 19 studies, but with probable publication bias, institutional settings and small samples ([Mehrabi 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12598933/)). Phone befriending is plausible, but a 2022 review found little research and no reliable effect size ([SSHRC brief](https://www.sshrc-crsh.gc.ca/society-societe/community-communite/ifca-iac/evidence_briefs-donnees_probantes/ksg_evidence_briefs/nelson-eng.aspx)). I found no independent trial showing an AI phone call reduces loneliness or deaths. ElliQ's trial (NCT03972787) has no published results found ([ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03972787)). A 2025 MIT/OpenAI study found heavy daily chatbot use correlated with more loneliness and dependence, though it is correlational ([arXiv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2503.17473)). The safe pilot claim is "reaches people, keeps them engaged, detects problems", not "cures loneliness". The size of the problem is real: WHO links loneliness to about 871,000 deaths a year (association, not proven cause) ([WHO](https://www.who.int/NEWS/ITEM/30-06-2025-SOCIAL-CONNECTION-LINKED-TO-IMPROVED-HEATH-AND-REDUCED-RISK-OF-EARLY-DEATH)), and in India about 13-15% of 60+ report frequent loneliness while another study reports about 36% (definitions differ).

**Retention.** Smart-speaker studies found most older users become non-users because they cannot find a valuable use ("Alexa is a Toy", [IU](https://scholarworks.indianapolis.iu.edu/items/10aaf444-3a20-4708-9e07-08064c84b137)); sustained use is tied to routine value like medicines, news and music. A call that rings the user removes the learning barrier. No month-6 or month-12 retention data exists for AI check-in services, and Indian voice-assistant use among older people was not studied.

**Risks and rules.** India has no companion-AI law in the notes. Abroad the direction is clear: California SB 243 (effective 1 January 2026) requires self-harm detection, crisis referral and "not human" reminders, and New York's law (effective 5 November 2025) covers systems that remember past chats and ask emotional questions, which a memory-keeping check-in could plausibly match [inference] ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/states-of-play/firms-navigating-ai-chatbot-risks-must-consider-regulatory-bumps)). China's rules require assessing dependence and offering easy exit, with details unverified ([Science and Technology Daily](https://www.stdaily.com/web/English/2026-07/28/content_554571.html)). Lawsuits so far concern minors and ChatGPT-type products; older-adult harm evidence is thin. CareCall's observed faults (overpromising, mishearing, over-attachment in Paro) are the failure modes your safety evaluation set should test. Not researched: EU AI Act treatment, consent for people with dementia, and family-monitoring privacy. The notes also warn that abuse is often by family, so alerts to a child can be unsafe in some homes.

**Design baseline (my judgement, not law in India):** say plainly it is an AI, never claim to be human, escalate crisis words to a human, nudge away from heavy use, let the senior say who gets alerts, and never promise a service you cannot do.

Two notes on the ranking. Opportunities 1, 2 and 6 can share one funnel and one customer, the NRI or metro child, so they are cheap to test together and form one product: a "parent safety line" with a scam shield and paid errands. Opportunities 3, 4, 5, 7 and 8 are the institutional route and give a path to scale without selling to thousands of individuals; start the conversations while the consumer tests run. A companion-only product (chat for loneliness) is not on the list on purpose: the evidence for AI-only loneliness benefit is weak, no one has shown they pay, and it carries the most regulatory exposure. Treat companionship as a feature of 1, not a product.

## 10. Verdict: a real need, a weak proven market, a small first business

**Is this a strong business?** Not yet, and not as a venture-scale consumer business on current evidence. It is a **good bet for a small, profitable service**, if two tests pass. For: the number of seniors will double by 2050; fraud losses are huge; carers are scarce; voice calls reach people that apps cannot; and you already have the product built. Against: no one has shown in India that children pay for AI check-ins; seniors themselves have little money; the funded players are human-heavy and lose money; the closest app, GenWise, shut; Amazon's USD 20 remote-caregiver plan was withdrawn; everyone abroad who made it work had a city, insurer or business payer; and a general AI model plus free tools (Truecaller, Google) press prices down [U]. The evidence on loneliness benefit is weak, so do not sell it as medicine. Realistic bar: a few hundred paying families at Rs 500-1,000 a month is a living for a solo founder with 2-3 part-timers. A large company needs an institutional payer.

**My recommendation, in order:** (1) Run tests 1 and 2 together in the same two weeks. (2) At the same time, send the pitches for tests 3, 4 and 7. (3) Skip hardware. (4) Get a lawyer's opinion on consent for an adult parent, call classification and health-claim wording before you scale beyond 100 users. (5) Keep a human-in-the-loop log from day one: it is both your safety record and your cost model.

**What would turn this into a "yes".** Test 1 gets 20% paid and parents still answer after a month; or one bank, insurer or employer signs a paid pilot; or a state or CSR programme buys by the senior; or a lawyer confirms an alert-only service is outside device rules and AI-voice check-ins are allowed under TRAI and DPDP. **What would make it a "no":** under 10% of the NRI and metro leads pay, answer rates under 50%, or the safety set shows unsafe replies you cannot fix. **What would worry me most:** the free-tool price floor in scams, and the human cost that sank Emoha's margins.

## Gaps and next evidence to collect

The notes were strong on demographics, fraud and problem prevalence, and weak on exactly what a founder needs. These are the real gaps, left unfilled on purpose: any survey of what Indian children or NRIs would pay; the number of NRI and metro-child households; WhatsApp, UPI and language use among 60+; insurer, employer and bank interest; hardware cost, certification and return rates; Indian consumer complaints about any player; Singapore, Taiwan and Nordic models; independent evidence for AI phone-call benefit; EU AI Act and dementia consent ethics; and primary legal texts for DPDP, TRAI and CDSCO (not read in full). Earlier repo notes (competitor scope and global trends) were used only as leads, and their unsourced prices and the YC "AI for the Aging Population" request (original text not obtained, only a third-party copy: [Modelence](https://modelence.com/yc-rfs-fall-2026/ai-for-the-aging-population)) are not treated as fact. The best next evidence is your own: 40 paid-pilot offers and 15 institutional pitches will tell you more than any further desk research.
